How the estimates work

These are Uniswap V3 pools discovered from the official factories. Supported stablecoins are USDC, USDT, DAI and USDbC where available. Other exchanges and V4 pools are not included.

Pool volume counts each swap input once over a complete recent day, marked at the reading’s UNI price. Pool fee totals estimate LP fees from swap inputs and the protocol fee split in effect for each swap; tiny per-swap rounding differences can remain. The recorded fee/volume period is shown separately from the current TVL reading.

Your estimate prefers recorded fee-growth counters. If archive access fails, a conservative estimate uses verified swaps without a liquidity-boundary crossing; trades with unknown liquidity are excluded. Usable recorded readings remain available for up to eight hours, with their original time shown. They are labeled Recorded rather than Live. It is not a backtest or guaranteed return.

Estimated fees and APR describe a full day while your position is in range, using the planned liquidity, recorded trading pace and today’s active pool liquidity, including dilution from your deposit. This is also shown for single-sided deposits waiting to enter. It does not measure the liquidity or trading that will exist at a future entry price. Time outside the range earns zero. APR uses the initial deposit value, without compounding or transaction costs.

UNI-only starts its minimum at the current price; stablecoin-only starts its maximum there. The usable entry rounds outward to Uniswap’s tick spacing so the deposit still needs only one token. You can adjust either price. A position outside its usable range earns zero fees at that reading; the separate in-range estimate is conditional on entry.

Prices are quoted in the paired stablecoin per UNI; dollar values use token prices at the reading and assume that stablecoin is worth $1. BNB Chain uses Binance-Peg tokens; Base USDT is a bridged token. Edge P&L compares position value at the chosen boundary with its value at deposit, excluding fees, gas and swaps. A lower boundary does not cap losses if UNI falls further.
